# October 20–November 2 pilot

Observation cutoff: November 2, 2026, 2:31:30 PM Jakarta (07:31:30 UTC).
Monday is incomplete. This is retrospective diagnosis, not an independent replay
or a claim that the model has been trained.

The frozen history contains 415 currency assessment versions. There are 376
matured delivered intervals: 288 have complete synchronized M1-close paths, 63
have missing path samples and 25 lack an eligible baseline or terminal minute.
Eight remain pending. Sixteen late corrections cannot reactivate an older call;
fifteen records have no direction. The raw seven-leg capture used verified
Pepperstone historical clocks and 105 sequential bridge requests in total.

Most early records lack a standalone basis and grade. Only 96 complete matured
standalone observations enter the direction-by-grade comparison, all using
diagnostic checkpoint/supersession windows rather than explicit prospective
evaluation horizons. Forty-one align and fifty-five oppose. On those same
intervals, the naive previous-direction comparator aligns forty-five times.
This pilot therefore does **not** demonstrate improved directional performance.
The intervals, currencies and triangulated crosses are correlated; these counts
are neither independent trades nor a net-profit test. The 32 currency-day means
are another descriptive view, not 32 independent experiments.

The four case reviews are in `../reviews.json`; their exact original snapshots,
outcome IDs and reasoning remain available through `learning case`. They review
saved reasoning and chronology, not independently reopened causal news evidence.
No directional hypothesis or statistical edge has been promoted. Three active
process lessons address expired stages, grade dominance with counterexamples,
and separation of policy foundations from session-dependent assumptions.

The machine-readable report and CSV preserve every measured case and its gaps.
New ordinary assessments freeze explicit evaluation horizons, opposition,
invalidation, mechanism tags, exact lesson versions and workflow hashes. Those
prospective cases are the appropriate next test of whether the revised process
improves decisions. Retain failures and successes; do not revise old verdicts to
fit charts or reward grade inflation.
